## The claim

> "AI now generates 75% of all new code at Google and has reached 94% adoption among engineering leaders"

**Source:** [FinancialContent (AB Newswire press release), September 3, 2026](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/abnewswire-2026-9-3-code-review-platforms-overwhelmed-as-ai-output-surges-500-faster-than-human-review)
**Who:** "Independent research firm Dunstan Research Group," in a report titled "The AI Code Review Paradox"

## Steelman

The direction is clearly right. Multiple 2026 surveys show broad AI coding adoption: Techreviewer's poll of 127 firms found 89% use AI to write or assist with code, with the median company reporting AI manages 26% to 50% of its codebase. Google-scale AI code generation in the majority range is plausible given internal tooling.

## What was demonstrated

- A press release asserts the numbers. No methodology, sample size, or definition of "new code" was published with it.
- The same report ranks a specific vendor's methodology as its top solution, which is worth knowing when reading its conclusions.
- The release itself carries a disclaimer: "We make no guarantees regarding its accuracy or completeness."

## What was claimed but not shown

- The 75% figure for Google is unattributed inside the release. No primary Google source is cited.
- "94% adoption among engineering leaders" has no disclosed sample, question wording, or definition of adoption.

## Verdict

**Unverifiable**

The numbers may be right, but a press release from an unfamiliar firm with no published methodology is not evidence. Per the house rule: thin evidence means Unverifiable, not Debunked.

## Why it matters

Unverifiable stats get laundered into "everyone knows" facts within weeks. The measured reality is more modest and more interesting than the headline numbers: METR's randomized trial found a 19% slowdown, Stanford's commit analysis shows a modest lift that depends on task type, and Google's DORA report models a J-curve with a verification tax. Self-reported adoption is real; measured productivity is a different question.
