# Audit: Ricursive Ripple-65B Preview "U.S. open-weight SOTA on agentic coding"

## The claim

> Ripple-65B Preview delivers strong results on agentic coding, achieving U.S. open-weight state-of-the-art (80.52) on TerminalBench 2.1 and surpassing significantly larger US open-weight models such as Reflection Beam 501B (80.1), Meta Muse Spark Open 816B (76.4), Thinking Machines Inkling 975B (63.8), and NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B (56.4).

**Source:** [Introducing Ripple-65B Preview, a Frontier Open-Weight Model, November 4, 2026](https://www.ricursive.com/ripple)
**Who:** Ricursive Intelligence, in its launch post for Ripple-65B Preview

## Steelman

The strongest fair reading is that a small, openly licensed dense model can now match or beat the best American open-weight models on agentic coding, and do it far more cheaply than the labs that came before. On this reading, Ricursive trained a 65 billion parameter model from scratch on 12 trillion tokens, published the weights under Apache 2.0 so anyone can download and run them, and posted 80.52 on TerminalBench 2.1 and 79.1 on SWE-bench Pro v2.0 Hard, leading every named U.S. open-weight rival on both. It did this, by its own accounting, for about $32 million in total R and D, which it estimates at one-tenth the cost of the previous open-weight leader. If that reading holds, the open-weight frontier is no longer a scale contest, and a 65B model that fits on a single workstation-class GPU is a genuine shift in who can build and run a top coding model.

## What was demonstrated

- **The weights are public, downloadable, and commercially licensed.** Ripple-65B Preview shipped on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0 on November 4, 2026, as a BF16 instruction-tuned model plus a base model, an NVFP4 quantized checkpoint, and a DFlash2 variant, with hosted access through Together AI, GMI Cloud, OpenRouter, and Fireworks. ([Ricursive, November 4, 2026](https://www.ricursive.com/ripple); [Hokai](https://hokai.io/hub/models/ripple-65b-preview); [Thaki Cloud](https://thakicloud.com/tech-blog/en/owm/ripple-65b-preview-open/))
- **The reported TerminalBench 2.1 score is 80.52, ahead of the four named rivals on that benchmark.** Ricursive's table lists Reflection Beam 501B at 80.1, Meta Muse Spark Open 816B at 76.4, Thinking Machines Inkling 975B at 63.8, and NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B at 56.4. ([Ricursive, November 4, 2026](https://www.ricursive.com/ripple); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/ex-googlers-new-apache-2-0-model-ripple-65b-preview-claims-the-u-s-open-coding-lead-on-two-major-benchmarks))
- **Ripple also leads its own table on SWE-bench Pro v2.0 Hard, at 79.1 against 76.5 for Beam and 72.4 for Muse Spark Open.** ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/ex-googlers-new-apache-2-0-model-ripple-65b-preview-claims-the-u-s-open-coding-lead-on-two-major-benchmarks); [AI Frontier Post](https://aifrontierpost.com/articles/ripple-65b-preview-open-coding-model/))
- **The model is small, dense, and cheap to serve relative to its rivals.** It is a 65B dense Transformer, trained from scratch on 12 trillion tokens, and Ricursive says it fits on hardware such as the NVIDIA DGX Spark. OpenRouter lists it at $0.20 per million input tokens and $0.60 per million output tokens. ([Ricursive, November 4, 2026](https://www.ricursive.com/ripple); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/ex-googlers-new-apache-2-0-model-ripple-65b-preview-claims-the-u-s-open-coding-lead-on-two-major-benchmarks))
- **The builders are a real chip-design lab with relevant pedigree.** Ricursive Intelligence was founded by Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini, who previously helped create Google's AlphaChip reinforcement learning system for chip design, and the company announced a $300 million Series A in January 2026 at a $4 billion valuation. ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/ex-googlers-new-apache-2-0-model-ripple-65b-preview-claims-the-u-s-open-coding-lead-on-two-major-benchmarks); [AI Frontier Post](https://aifrontierpost.com/articles/ripple-65b-preview-open-coding-model/))

## What was claimed but not shown

- **No independent party has reproduced the 80.52 score.** A week after release there is no Artificial Analysis entry and no third-party reproduction of the headline number; every benchmark figure in circulation is Ricursive's own. ([Eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/ripple-65b-review); [OrcaRouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/ripple-65b-preview-launch-review); [Hokai](https://hokai.io/hub/models/ripple-65b-preview))
- **The lead over Beam is 0.4 points on one benchmark, and Ripple trails elsewhere in its own table.** Ripple leads only 3 of the 22 rows in its own comparison table, the two TerminalBench rows and SWE-bench Pro v2.0 Hard, while Beam leads on five coding and agent tests. A 0.4 point gap on an agent benchmark is well inside harness noise. ([Eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/ripple-65b-review); [IBL](https://ibl.ai/blog/ripple-65b-dense-open-weight-coding-model-what-the-benchmarks-say); [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-ripple-65b-edges-a-501b-model-80-5-to-80-1-on-terminal-bench/))
- **The scores are sensitive to the harness, and the launch materials are not consistent about them.** Ricursive's own model card says TerminalBench 2.1 scores were lower under the official Terminus 2 harness for every model except Muse Spark Open, and reviewers note the launch chart and the model card disagree on some competitor scores. ([Eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/ripple-65b-review); [IBL](https://ibl.ai/blog/ripple-65b-dense-open-weight-coding-model-what-the-benchmarks-say))
- **"Surpassing significantly larger models" is true on total parameters and misleading on active compute.** Beam is a mixture of experts model with about 23 billion active parameters, so Ripple's dense 65B activates more parameters per token than every model it is compared with. The size framing compares a dense model's total against MoE totals. ([IBL](https://ibl.ai/blog/ripple-65b-dense-open-weight-coding-model-what-the-benchmarks-say); [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-ripple-65b-edges-a-501b-model-80-5-to-80-1-on-terminal-bench/))
- **The "U.S." qualifier does a lot of quiet work.** The claim excludes DeepSeek, Qwen, and every other non-U.S. lab, so it is a claim about the best open-weight model trained inside the United States, not the best open-weight model. ([OrcaRouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/ripple-65b-preview-launch-review))
- **The $32 million "one-tenth the cost" figure is Ricursive's own estimate, built from the other side's public statements.** The launch page says the figure is estimated "to the best of our knowledge," and the one-tenth comparison is built from Reflection's reported compute commitments and training durations, not matched disclosures from both companies. ([Ricursive, November 4, 2026](https://www.ricursive.com/ripple); [AI Frontier Post](https://aifrontierpost.com/articles/ripple-65b-preview-open-coding-model/))
- **The advertised 1 million token context does not survive contact with the hosted API.** The model card advertises context up to 1M tokens, but the first commercial endpoint carrying the same checkpoint lists a 16,384 token limit, with a 14,745 token output ceiling. ([OrcaRouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/ripple-65b-preview-launch-review); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/ex-googlers-new-apache-2-0-model-ripple-65b-preview-claims-the-u-s-open-coding-lead-on-two-major-benchmarks); [Eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/ripple-65b-review))
- **It is a Preview, and the company says post-training was light.** Reviewers note the name signals a first cut rather than a final release, so the numbers may move in either direction before a non-preview version lands. ([AI Frontier Post](https://aifrontierpost.com/articles/ripple-65b-preview-open-coding-model/); [Thaki Cloud](https://thakicloud.com/tech-blog/en/owm/ripple-65b-preview-open/))

## Verdict

**Overstated**

Something real sits underneath: a genuinely strong 65B dense coding model, trained from scratch, with public weights under Apache 2.0 and competitive scores on two agentic coding benchmarks in its own table. But the headline claim runs ahead of the evidence. The state of the art margin is 0.4 points on a self-reported benchmark with no independent reproduction a week after release, Ripple trails its rivals on most rows of its own table, the "10x larger" framing ignores that Beam activates only about 23 billion parameters per token, and the 1M context and one-tenth cost figures both carry qualifiers the headline drops. A fairer headline is that a small American lab shipped a strong open coding model at an unusually low reported cost, and the leaderboard still needs an independent referee.

## Why it matters

If Ripple's numbers hold up under independent testing, the economics of frontier-class coding models change: a 65B dense model that anyone can download and run on workstation hardware undercuts the assumption that top coding performance needs a half-trillion parameter model and a datacenter budget. That is exactly why the framing matters. A 0.4 point self-reported lead, presented as a settled state of the art, will be quoted as fact by buyers, investors, and rival labs long before anyone re-runs the benchmark. The weights are public, so the verification is cheap and anyone can do it. Until someone does, the honest version is narrower and still interesting: a promising open model with a real artifact, a thin margin, and a leaderboard that has not yet been independently checked.
